## Section I — Rewriting the Epic: Dopdi Mejhen vs. Classical Sanskrit Myth

> **[Author's Entry Section I — Student Essay Prompt (~200–250 words)]**
> Replace this block in `src/content/essays/02-subaltern-body-battlefield.md` with your opening analysis contrasting the *Mahabharata*'s royal Draupadi with Mahasweta Devi's Santhal revolutionary Dopdi Mejhen (*Draupadi*, 1978, trans. Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak).
>
> - **Focus Point A:** Engage with Gayatri Spivak's critical framing of the subaltern woman and the figure of Senanayak as the pluralist, metropolitan specialist who studies the tribal rebel in order to apprehend her.
> - **Focus Point B:** Examine how Devi shifts the site of epic struggle from royal courtly honor to indigenous land, water rights, and anti-feudal insurgency in Naxalbari.

## Section II — The Unclothed Body as Counter-Weapon

> **[Author's Entry Section II — Student Essay Prompt (~250 words)]**
> Replace this block in `src/content/essays/02-subaltern-body-battlefield.md` with your analysis of the final scene of *Draupadi*, where Dopdi refuses to put on her clothes in front of Senanayak.
>
> - **Focus Point A:** Analyze how Dopdi turns custodial sexual violence—intended by the state apparatus to silence and humiliate—into a terrifying indictment of military masculinity.
> - **Focus Point B:** Explain why Senanayak's theoretical and tactical mastery collapses before her unarmed, unashamed question: *"What more can you do?"*
